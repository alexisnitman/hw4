# Problem 10 design improvements

## Boutique visual direction

I changed the display typography to a more expressive editorial serif and shifted the palette toward Yale blue, silver, and baby blue. A subtle pattern of faded Ys adds brand texture without competing with products. This makes the shop feel more like a considered boutique than a generic catalogue.

## Moving home collage

The home page now uses an animated collage of catalogue product photos, with a stylized Yale-blue campus scene layered into the composition. The movement creates visual discovery and gives shoppers a reason to stay on the landing page.

## Softer rounded interface

I rounded product cards, buttons, filters, forms, chat panels, and image frames. The softer shapes make the site feel more welcoming and less rigid, which supports comfortable browsing and clearer calls to action.

## Virtual try-on decision

The earlier virtual try-on preview was removed because it did not provide a convincing enough representation of how clothing would look on a person. Removing a misleading feature protects shopper trust; the site now focuses on reliable product images, descriptions, prices, and stock information.

## Friendlier chat

The chat greeting and status language now sound more encouraging and personal. The assistant invites shoppers to explore and reassures them that it can help find the right piece, making the shopping interaction feel more like boutique service.
